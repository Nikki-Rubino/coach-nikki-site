Hey,

If we're lucky enough, a lot of our lives may have been documented by photos, videos, articles and so forth. But what if you had access to your own thoughts, memories, feelings, accomplishments and interactions 1, 5, 10 or 20 years down the track?

Here I am admitting that, as a 39-year-old, my TikTok feed (yes, I'm on yet another platform of procrastination) has recently been filled with glow-ups of Harry Styles, from the young teen in One Direction to the outstanding human he is today. It made me think about how lucky he and the One Direction fellas are that so much of their journey and accomplishments were actually documented.

Have you ever stumbled across something from the past while cleaning out your closet that left you feeling nostalgic, thinking about how far you've come? Journaling can give you insight into what's serving you, what isn't, what makes you feel good, what you avoid, and what next steps you need to take. My younger self used to document on a daily basis. I can't remember when or why it stopped — I think maybe when I came back from living in London and started working in advertising in 2006. Life becomes a little more hectic, and now that I think back, my anxiety levels have increased every year since.

Seven common benefits that can result from starting to journal:

- **Achieving goals** — knowing what you're working towards keeps you accountable on the days you find it harder.
- **Witnessing your progress and growth** — all it takes is a small win or the realisation that something is better engrained than before.
- **Self-confidence increases** — look back and feel proud of the accomplishments you achieved or the lessons you learned.
- **Enhanced writing and communication skills** — we get better at the things we do consistently. This blog itself is a challenge I set for myself at the end of 2021. I have so much self-doubt about writing, and as a super shy, quiet kid I was always the observer in the room. This blog is forcing me to say things to a wider audience.
- **Reduced stress and anxiety** — does anyone relate to that little voice that says some not-so-nice things on loop? If we can stop for a minute and dump that worry onto paper, we can sort through what's actually happening and what needs attention.
- **Get inspired** — let the unconscious mind run free. So much of our time we need our logical, critical brains; writing can release problem-solving and inner visions, and you never know what your brain will put on paper.
- **Bulletproof your memory** — writing something down lets your brain know you want to remember it.

So you know some benefits, and you feel you want them. But where do you start? How about you try this each week for a month. Choose a day, time and location where you can allocate 15 to 30 minutes. Find an inspiring writing utensil or format that works for you, then sit and answer these simple questions — be as brief or descriptive as you want:

- Who made you feel good?
- What did you do this week that moved you closer to your goals?
- Is there anything you wish you'd done differently?
- What did you most enjoy doing this week?
- How did you procrastinate on important tasks?
- What did you learn?
- What's the funniest thing that happened to you?

I'd love to know what you find out, accomplish, or simply share your inspiring journal cover!

Until next week,
Nikki
