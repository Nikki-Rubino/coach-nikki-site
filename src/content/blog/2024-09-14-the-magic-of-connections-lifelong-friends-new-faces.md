Hey,

Ever felt a little intimidated by the idea of bringing different circles of friends together? Let me share a story…

A while back, I had this brilliant idea to organise a dinner with some of the amazing women I train. All strong, smart, fabulous — what could go wrong, right? But then my brain kicked into what-if overdrive. "What if they don't vibe?" "What if someone has a food intolerance I didn't account for?" "What if I turn into a coaching robot and forget how to have a regular conversation outside of fitness?" You get the picture.

For weeks, I procrastinated. I was used to training these ladies, not being the social organiser! Meanwhile, I'd already scheduled a quick hangout with my high school friends in, like, 20 minutes. Boom — date set, venue sorted, and I knew the conversation would just flow.

And that got me thinking: why is it easier with some friendships and trickier with others? The reality is, whether you've known someone for decades or just a few months, building a sense of community is something we all need, even if it feels a bit daunting sometimes.

**Why human connection is key.** Humans are social creatures. We thrive on connection — whether it's with your lifelong BFF who remembers your terrible high school hair choices, or with the new people who light you up with shared interests and goals. These relationships provide the support, laughter, and sense of belonging that make life richer.

**The beauty of longtime friends.** Old friends are like a comfy pair of sneakers — reliable, familiar, and always there for you. There's something magical about how effortlessly the conversation flows; it's like time just picks up where it left off.

**New friendships = new adventures.** But let's not forget the excitement of meeting new people who just get you. Whether it's through a shared love of fitness, hobbies, or goals, these new friendships bring fresh perspectives and endless possibilities.

Spoiler alert: I eventually organised that dinner. It was perfect. Everyone hit it off, laughed, and shared stories — it reminded me how important it is to get out of my head and just make space for connection.

So, whether you're catching up with an old friend or meeting someone new at your next class, remember that these connections aren't just nice-to-haves — they're essential. Keep nurturing those bonds, old and new.

Take care and stay Sassy,
Nikki
