Hey,

I want to start by expressing my immense pride in all of you this week!

It's been a week of remarkable breakthroughs, enlightening "aha" moments, and substantial strength gains. We've witnessed individuals conquering their first official push-ups, achieving ease with floor exercises that once drew sarcastic glances, and our Sassy Seniors have excelled in building their confidence and independence getting in and out of chairs, all while experiencing significant improvements in their overall mobility — reduced pressure on knees, less pain in necks and shoulders, and newfound ease walking long distances. Our Animal Flow enthusiasts have also united to create an inspiring, vibrant community.

Now, let's delve into this week's topic. Have you ever taken a moment to truly observe how your body adapts to your daily environment? Consider the positions you find yourself in while working, sleeping and driving. Which side do you typically carry your bag or your child? How do you get up and down from the floor?

For those familiar with my journey, you know I once spent long hours seated at a desk with four screens around me — three in front, and the client screen behind me to my right. I would frequently twist my body to the right while seated. This wreaked havoc on my right shoulder, and it was all happening while I was still under 30!

It wasn't until I underwent a movement screen that I realised I couldn't rotate to the left. My right shoulder was significantly higher than the left, and my left hip and glute weren't functioning as they should. Looking back, I could trace these issues all the way to my school days. I carried my backpack over my right shoulder, thinking it was the "cool" way to wear it, and often sat with my left leg tucked underneath me, leaning to my right side. These habits carried into my work life, where I'd sit with my left leg crossed over the right, shifting my weight to the right, rotating only to the right to view my screens, and carrying my 12kg laptop bag exclusively over my right shoulder. Over time, these seemingly minor habits added up to serious strength imbalances, pain, and a lack of trust in my own body.

The silver lining is that once you become aware of these daily positions, you can take steps to address them. I urge you all to pay close attention to the positions you find yourself in daily. Are there alternative positions you can adopt? For desk workers, consider spending just five minutes opening up your chest and shoulders, and take moments to look up or away from your screen. Did you play sports that favoured a dominant kicking leg or throwing arm? That likely means one side of your body is strong and coordinated, while the other is more mobile but less strong. When you need to get down to the floor, do you always rely on one particular leg to support you?

Awareness is the first step, and I'd love to hear what you observe about your own daily environments.

Until next week, stay Sassy,
Nikki
