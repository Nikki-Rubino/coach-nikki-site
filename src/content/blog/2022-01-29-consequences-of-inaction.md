So you've set out some intentions or goals, whatever you choose to call them. You have a breakdown, a simple plan of steps to achieve it, a time and location, and an accountability system. Now the final piece of the puzzle to actually going through with it all and staying on track is the consequence of inaction.

What will be your consequence of not doing what you said you were going to do? Thinking of the long game — if it's a lifestyle habit we're trying to change, what will happen if these actions aren't addressed in 5, 10 or 15 years to come? What will happen to me if I don't abide by my set times of going for that walk, swim, mobility sequence, or playtime with my kids? That should give some perspective on why you need to commit to these small, simple tasks daily or weekly.

James Clear addresses a habit contract in *Atomic Habits*:

- What is your main objective?
- If you don't follow through, what is the consequence?
- Who will you report to?
- Your signature, partner's signature, date.

This can be a written or verbal agreement. I personally had a similar thing with my own accountability coach last year, working towards some scary goals. I had one task each day: text through three action tasks each morning by a time I'd chosen, then text again once one or more were completed, with until midnight to do so. My consequence — something I came up with and agreed to — was that if I didn't complete one task off the list that day, I was not allowed to train for two days! So the consequence also has to mean something to you.

Go forth and create your habit contract, whether it's to yourself (don't break the chain) or with your accountability buddy. It's amazing what can be achieved with a little clarity, focus and a plan.
