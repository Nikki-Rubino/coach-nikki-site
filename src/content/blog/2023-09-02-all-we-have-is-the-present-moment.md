Hey,

You know those weeks where life just seems to start piling up? There are things on your to-do list that you've set aside time for, then things out of your control happen. Time gets lost, you physically start to unravel, and the universe seems to be testing you in every way.

Last week I wrote about resilience and community. This week, I was a by-product of it all:

- Taking on extra coaching sessions needed as others were out of action.
- Eating a chicken wrap that left me with violent food poisoning while out on the road, 90 minutes before a three-hour coaching stint in venues outside my usual gym.
- Falling asleep on the floor twice — once mid-training session with other coaches, the other during a weekly visit back to my folks' house.
- A family member unexpectedly in hospital.

Every day there seemed to be a new challenge, a new mental or physical drain, while still trying to get "life" done. It's a reminder that during these times, we only have the present moment. Ask yourself: what can I do right now that is in my control? Who do I have in my corner for help?

The unexpected chicken wrap dilemma gave me courage to ask strangers where the nearest bathroom was, and to problem-solve how I was actually going to trudge through (hello, Chemist Warehouse, and luckily time to nap in the back of my car during the freak storm that rolled in). During the coaches' session, I was encouraged to face one of my fears: getting upside down on the "big boy" rings, the ones that hang from the ceiling. Something that, when alone, I don't have the courage to try. Sometimes we can't see how strong we actually are — it may take the encouragement of others who believe in us and see us from a different perspective. With two coaches by my side encouraging me the whole way, I proved to myself that I can do it. Don't get me wrong, I was shaking like a leaf with tears welling in my eyes the whole time I was upside down!

The morning after a loved one was in hospital, I was meant to have a jam-packed day. I knew I wouldn't be able to offer my full attention, or trust myself driving a far distance. Cancelling sessions is not something I do lightly, but I had comfort knowing all my 1:1s would understand completely. My early morning was meant to be the biggest attendance in my Animal Flow class, in the happy place of Jungle Botany. I knew both of these would fill my own cup and I could feed off the surrounding vibes to pick myself up — being totally transparent with my crew and saying, hey, I'm not 100% this morning and I'm simply trying my best not to stuff up.

Know yourself, your limits, who you can turn to for help, and how you can raise your own vibes. It's okay to be vulnerable; we are merely human. Life has ups and downs and freaking curveballs — but you are stronger than you even realise.

Until next week, stay Sassy,
Nikki
