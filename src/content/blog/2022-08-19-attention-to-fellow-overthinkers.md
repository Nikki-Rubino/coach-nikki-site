Hi Friend,

Have you heard the saying, "Learn from the past, live in the present, plan for the future"? As someone who tends to be very anxious, I find it difficult to simply *be*. I admire people who can sit in silence, just breathe, and look so content doing it.

I used to think something was wrong with me — I need music or noise to concentrate, I need a to-do list but get distracted trying to create it, I fidget constantly, and unless I'm fully engaged I zone out. Forever thinking about the what-ifs, playing out every scenario in my head and planning for each — it's exhausting. On further research, I'm not the only one who does this. Can you relate?

Being mid-transition (selling our apartment, trying to crack the brutal Sydney rental market, still seeing clients, packing up, living in an empty apartment like a squatter with a racing mind) left me feeling lost and frazzled.

> So how can an overactive mind start to calm down?

Take a moment and try **"Engaging the 5"** — a moment to be present with your surroundings, using your five senses:

- What can you see?
- What can you hear?
- What can you smell?
- What can you feel?
- What can you taste?

Sometimes I'll have my journal with me, but if I don't, I take a walk outside, close my eyes, and — to keep my fidgety self happy — rub each finger for each sense to focus on. Instead of writing them down, I just say them in my head.

If you've got a wandering, overthinking mind, try this a few times this week and let me know how you go. I'll leave you with a quote that resonated:

> "If you are depressed you are living in the past. If you are anxious you are living in the future. If you are at peace you are living in the present." — Lao Tzu

Until next week, hang in there,
Nikki
