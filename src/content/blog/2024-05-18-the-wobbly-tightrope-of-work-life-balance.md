Hey,

I wanted to share a little confession: despite my best efforts and all my preaching about the importance of work-life balance and movement, I've been feeling like a bit of a fraud lately. Over the past six months, I took on two courses that demanded a huge chunk of my mental bandwidth. One of them resulted in my first ever presentation experience, which brought all of my insecurities to the surface. My priorities shifted, and as a result my movement patterns and personal balance have been way out of whack.

So if any of you have felt similarly off-kilter, just know you're not alone. Balancing everything can feel like walking a tightrope, and sometimes it's okay to wobble (or even take an unexpected tumble).

Here are three tips I'm embracing to help regain my priorities and get back on track.

**Prioritise self-compassion.** It's easy to be hard on ourselves when things aren't perfect, but remember that being kind to yourself is crucial. Acknowledge that it's okay to have periods where balance isn't achievable and that you're doing the best you can. My own negative thoughts can shout loud — getting out into new surroundings, ideally in sunshine, can help bring me back to the here and now.

**Schedule "me time".** Block out specific times in your calendar for self-care and movement, just like you would for any important meeting or deadline. This helps ensure your personal time doesn't get overshadowed by work or other commitments. My first step was to book in an appointment with Crystal, our resident JB massage therapist — niggles were coming back with a vengeance that I couldn't get rid of myself.

**Set small, achievable goals.** Instead of aiming for perfect balance all at once, start with small, manageable steps. Whether it's a quick daily walk, a 10-minute stretch, or just taking a few moments to breathe deeply, these little actions accumulate to make a big difference. I vow to do 15 minutes of mobility each morning to combat my sleeping patterns.

Remember, striving for balance is a journey, not a destination. It's perfectly normal to have phases where things are a bit chaotic. What matters is recognising when we're off course and gently guiding ourselves back.

Here's to embracing the wobble and finding our way back to steadier ground together!

Until next week, stay Sassy,
Nikki
