Hey,

So you feel your body has a few little niggles that you can't iron out yourself, but you don't know where to turn for help.

Firstly, I want to preface this with two things.

Movement is medicine. Your body needs and thrives with movement. Those niggles can be due to lack of joint nourishment from simply not moving and being strong in various angles.

Not all practitioners are the same. If someone isn't assessing you, giving you things to correct at home, and is simply spending 15 minutes with you and making you book in again, it may not be the right fit.

As mentioned last week, my own balance was out of whack. First up, I booked in with Crystal, our resident massage therapist at JB's, for my shoulder, elbow and low back release and cupping. All of us coaches get tune ups from her, and we encourage our clients to see her too. Why keep it in the JB family? Because we talk to each other. We check in after our clients have seen her to see if we need to amend or look out for anything in our sessions. I also had my six-month chiro adjustment. (My sleep position is nowhere near optimal, even worse when I'm stressed.) Then I hate to admit how silly I was to keep my own PT handstand session an hour after my adjustment and start my new program the following day — needless to say, my lower back is rebelling against me and seized up as I write this on a Friday night.

So many times I've tried to collaborate with an outside physio and chiro about a person we both have sessions with, and they simply don't want to work together. There have been many instances where people have gone to a physio, been given homework without an understanding of why or how to do the movement properly — a simple "watch the video and be on your way". So our next session is spent delving into that to make sure they know how and why they've been prescribed these exercises. I believe everyone should have an understanding of their own body, how to control it, and who to turn to when something doesn't feel okay.

So how do we know who to turn to? My simple answer is:

- **Massage:** for brain and body release.
- **Physio:** for muscle injury, tendon, or chronic issues.
- **Chiro:** for bones, structural and joint issues.

Let's delve a little deeper.

**Massage** is effective for relieving muscle tension, reducing stress and promoting relaxation. It can help alleviate pain from chronic conditions like fibromyalgia, headaches and general muscle soreness, enhances blood flow to aid healing, and is useful for recovery from soft tissue injuries like strains and sprains.

**Physio** is essential for recovery from surgeries, fractures and sports injuries. It's useful for managing chronic conditions like arthritis, back pain and respiratory issues, helps in regaining strength and mobility after surgery, and is beneficial for conditions affecting movement and coordination.

**Chiro** is ideal for issues related to spinal misalignment, effective for chronic or acute back and neck pain, and can help with joint pain and conditions like sciatica.

Choosing the right therapy depends on the specific health issues you are experiencing. Often, these therapies can complement each other, providing a comprehensive approach to health and wellbeing. Find someone who assesses you, who you feel comfortable with, and who you can ask relevant questions — so you come out of the session not only feeling great but knowing what they worked on.
