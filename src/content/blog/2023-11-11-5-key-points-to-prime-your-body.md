Hey,

This week I dove into the crucial topic of priming your body before a training session, during a workshop with fellow coaches. The question on everyone's mind: "Should you prime your body before every workout?" The answers varied among us, reflecting diverse ages, clientele and training backgrounds.

One coach was a definitive "no", another believed it doesn't overly matter, and I'm firmly in the "hell yes!" camp. Here's the thing — there's no one-size-fits-all answer. Our bodies are unique, each with its own history and story. Factors like training history, sleep quality and daily stresses play a significant role in determining the need for priming.

I believe that understanding your body is key. You need a way to assess and prime based on how you feel at that specific moment. For instance, moving in the morning versus after a full day's work can yield different results. Here are five key points to consider before any workout, regardless of age:

- **Quality of sleep:** did you get a good night's sleep?
- **Stress levels:** how are you feeling stress-wise?
- **Nutrition:** what has your body been fuelled with in the last 36 to 48 hours?
- **Niggles:** any joint or muscle discomfort?
- **Hydration:** are you staying hydrated?

Create a simple movement "check-in system" with three to five basic movements to connect your brain and body. It's your way of letting your body answer what it needs — whether that's gentle movement, soft tissue work, or a quick readiness to dive into your workout.

While achieving all these factors 100% of the time is a challenge, having a pre-movement check-in routine allows your body to guide you. A great coach recognises what a person is ready for and adjusts the session accordingly. It's about not just seeing it, but also teaching individuals what to look for in themselves.

As I hit my 40s, I've shifted my training approach. I move differently, but the result is clear: stronger than ever before, better mobility, fewer niggles. Understanding your body is a powerful tool that adapts to changes in your environment, hormones and cognitive function.

Personally, I've found Animal Flow to be a fantastic way to connect with my breath, brain, body and energy levels. It primes my joints, assesses my range of motion, and guides me on what I need before each session. It's not just for me; components of Animal Flow are incorporated into my clients' warm-up processes to enhance their self-awareness.

Stay active and connected with your body — you never know, you may actually inspire someone else to do the same.
