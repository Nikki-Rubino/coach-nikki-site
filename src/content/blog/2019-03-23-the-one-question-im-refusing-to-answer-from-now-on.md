Hey,

I'm usually an open book — ask me anything and I'll give you an honest answer. Except for one question, and from now on I'll simply answer "nope, not going there, next question."

I want to open with this: you never know another person's story. Unless you're close enough to them, and they're open enough to share it, you don't know what's going on behind the scenes.

So let me ask — why is it that if you're married and over 30, this is just deemed a relevant question? "So when are you having kids?" "When will you make so-and-so happy grandparents?" There are so many things about this question that make my blood boil:

1. How does it affect you when, and if, I have kids?
2. What if I had actually been trying for years and failing every month?
3. Why is it deemed my duty to bear them at all?
4. What if I'd found out I actually can't?

Yes, I know my own age. Yes, I realise how long I've been with my husband. Yes, my folks would make awesome grandparents. Yes, I'm aware I'm now the only chance they have. But it's number two that gripes me. I have witnessed three people I consider close go through the hell and heartbreak of trying, failing, and trying over and over again — the physical, emotional and financial pain of reaching a possibility only to lose it weeks later. What if behind the scenes this is going on, and you come along and, just because you have nothing else in common to ask, bring up that subject — which for them is heartbreaking?

And number three: why is it a girl's only duty to have a kid anyway? Some people just want to be a mum — that's great. Some want to build a business, to make something to leave behind. I've been lucky enough to travel, have adventures, and create lasting memories with people I love — adventures I wouldn't have had if I was at home worrying about school lunches. I've changed careers and feel like I'm actually making a difference. Why isn't that enough? To be honest, the whole thing terrifies me — I hate hospitals, have no pain threshold, and have never really been around kids.

That little question you may deem innocent makes me want to tell you to mind your own business. If you're asking because you feel you have no other subject to discuss, here are some ideas: tell me what you've been up to, tell me about your travels, anything health and fitness, TV shows or podcasts you're into, penguins (I love those little fellas), or something you actually want to know the answer to that's relevant.

So that's it — I'm done with any reference to this question unless I bring it up myself. Think about how your questions and comments may make someone feel.

Until next time,
Nikki
