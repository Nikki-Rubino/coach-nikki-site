Hey,

I want to give you my top 10 tips that helped me learn to embrace running. No, I don't love it, but I do love a sense of accomplishment!

Every year there seems to be a new kind of fun run — City2Surf, Sutherland to Surf, the Colour Run, even novelty events such as zombie runs. So you want to participate but have no idea where to start? That little inner voice talks you out of it: "I'm not a runner", "I could never do it", "I'm not fit enough". Well, I'm here to say yes, you can! Tell that inner voice to bugger off and let your inner cheerleader shine through.

I've been there. I hated running — when my hubby would ask me to join him, I'd feel so much slower and thumpier. He looked like a chiselled gazelle floating along the pavement, while I had legs rubbing together, out of breath, face like a beetroot, and we'd barely started. Since then I've completed many 5km fun runs, the 11km Sutherland to Surf, the 14km City2Surf, 15km in the Wings for Life run in Melbourne, and my own personal best — my first and probably last half marathon. Never in my dreams did I think I could jog 21km.

**1. Invest in shoes.** Not just the fancy-looking kind — ones that are right for you. Go somewhere like a specialist running store that does a free stride assessment. (Looking into how to strengthen and stimulate your feet is well worth it too.)

**2. Grab a buddy.** What better way to stay motivated and accountable than buddying up with a friend — the same fitness level trying it out, or someone who'll give you that little push.

**3. Make a fire-up playlist.** Make a list of favourite tracks at the right speed for you. I start with a steady pace for a few tracks, then when I'm getting tired I throw in a fire-up song, then head back to steady tracks to finish.

**4. Set a specific goal.** It doesn't matter how small — a 5km fun run, City2Surf, or half/full marathon territory. Own it, commit to it, and go for it.

**5. Strengthen that booty and hamstrings.** Running is cardio, but you still need strength to power you and avoid injury. Think activations — beast and crab, walking lunges, squats and deadlifts. A resistance band with X-walks, hip thrusts, frog pumps and single-leg RDLs is a simple place to start.

**6. Release your hip flexors and hip area.** If you sit all day, these get super tight. When you run, you need a range of motion that allows a good stride. Think yoga poses like pigeon, reaching X-walks, or from Animal Flow, scorpion reaches and front steps. (For the release to be effective, the bum squeeze is essential.)

**7. Run the same route.** Walk or drive the area first and choose a route you feel comfortable with. If there's a massive hill that makes you anxious, avoid it while starting out — use it as a challenge later.

**8. Take notice of this route.** The first time, look for landmarks: a tree, park, sign, shop. Give yourself mini landmark goals to jog to, and mark them along the route, so eventually you jog the whole way with minimal walking breaks.

**9. Go for it.** Jog as far as you can until you need to walk. When you slow, notice where you got to, congratulate yourself, and you've now set a benchmark to beat next time.

**10. Run for a cause.** What better motivation than knowing you're helping make a difference? Plenty of runs let you raise money for a charity. Find something you're passionate about — if people are sponsoring you, there's more accountability to do your best.

"You don't have to see the whole staircase, you just have to take that first step." — Martin Luther King Jr

Go on, get out there and mark your route!

Nikki
