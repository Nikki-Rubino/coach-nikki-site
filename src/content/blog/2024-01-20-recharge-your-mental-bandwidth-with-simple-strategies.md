Today, we're diving into the fascinating world of mental bandwidth and why it's crucial to recognise when it's running low. Just like a smartphone needs a recharge, our brains need a break too.

Mental bandwidth is like the brain's data capacity. It's the mental space we use for decision-making, problem-solving and everyday tasks. When it's overloaded, we can feel stressed, tired and less productive. After a few discussions this week, I wasn't alone in having low mental capacity. Moving house, not by choice, threw a spanner in the works over the past month. External stresses — moving, big work projects, school holidays, caring for family, any added pressure — can cause some havoc.

Signs of low mental bandwidth: forgetfulness, difficulty concentrating, increased irritability, procrastination.

I'll admit, this week, upon waking in a new environment only 200m down the road, even the simple things seemed overwhelming. Where are the light switches in the dark? Where are the power points? What are those noises? Will my phone hotspot be enough to run virtual sessions? After almost having a mild panic attack and simply wanting to cry, I took a few deep breaths and noticed: what is one thing giving me the ick right now, and how can I fix it? The stuffiness of the place was in my face from being locked up, so task one was simply to open the balcony door and get some fresh air. Problem solved. Next.

Some simple recharge hacks for your awesome brain:

**Mindful breathing.** Inhale deeply, hold, exhale slowly. This simple practice can calm your mind and boost mental clarity.

**Mini breaks.** Schedule short breaks throughout the day. Step away from your desk, stretch, or take a quick walk. Physical activity enhances cognitive function. Dance party in the kitchen, anyone?

**Brain dump.** When your mind feels cluttered, jot down your thoughts on paper. This can clear mental space for more important tasks.

**Prioritise tasks.** Break your day into manageable tasks. Tackle high-priority items when your mental energy is at its peak.

And for a bandwidth-boosting lifestyle: aim for seven to nine hours of quality sleep, limit screen time (especially before bed — I'm guilty of the doom scroll, slowly switching to a short gratitude journal and a read), stay hydrated and well-nourished, and learn to say no when necessary. Overcommitting depletes your mental bandwidth — no apology required.

Remember, recognising low mental bandwidth is the first step towards a healthier, more productive you. Wishing you a mentally recharged week ahead!
