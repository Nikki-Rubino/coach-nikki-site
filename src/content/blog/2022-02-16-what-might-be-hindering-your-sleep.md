Hey,

So how did you go analysing your own sleep patterns? Did anything come to light for you?

Full disclosure: many things can disrupt our sleep, some we can change or improve. But there are two main things we maybe can't control — shift work, and a newborn baby. These will definitely hinder sleep quality and are a whole other area to touch on, so if this is you, take what info resonates at this point in time.

The amount of sleep is important, as well as the quality of the sleep itself. There are many sleep trackers out there if you want to delve further but, in a nutshell, how do you feel you can get through tasks the following day? If we're tired, brain fog is real. How many good decisions do you make when you're tired? When sleep deprived, we tend to:

- Lose cognitive function (ever bumped into a coffee table that's been there forever?).
- Get increased hunger and appetite, reaching for calorie-rich foods.
- Get decreased feelings of satisfaction after eating (eat the whole packet instead of a few bites).
- Lose the ability to focus (re-reading things, or not having the capacity to start them).
- Potentially be a danger behind a moving vehicle.
- Have poor decision-making ability.
- Increase your likelihood of infection, and reduce your response to the flu vaccine.

The list goes on, but you see where I'm going. So how can we improve the quality of sleep? Take a look at this list from the National Institute of Health and see which ones are a culprit in your evening:

- Stick to a sleep schedule.
- Exercise is great, but not too late in the day.
- Avoid caffeine and nicotine.
- Avoid alcoholic drinks before bed.
- Avoid large meals and beverages late at night.
- If possible, avoid medicines that delay or disrupt your sleep.
- Don't take naps after 3pm.
- Relax before bed — don't overschedule your day so there's no time left for unwinding. A relaxing activity like reading or listening to music should be part of your bedtime ritual.
- Take a hot bath before bed.
- Dark bedroom, cool bedroom, gadget-free bedroom.
- Get outside in natural daylight for at least 30 minutes each day; if possible, wake up with the sun or use bright lights in the morning.
- Don't lie in bed awake.

Take this week to analyse what might be hindering your sleep quality. Next week I'll be delving into ideas for your own sleep routine.
