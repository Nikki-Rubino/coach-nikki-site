Hey,

Imagine life as a grand recipe book, filled with countless combinations of movements and experiences waiting to be explored. Just as a chef combines ingredients to create a dish, we gather and process information to develop our understanding of the world around us.

Reflect for a moment on the movements and activities that shaped your childhood. Perhaps you recall the exhilaration of dancing, the thrill of sport, or the simple joy of running and playing. Me, I loved to climb our jacaranda tree. These early experiences lay the foundation for the recipes of movement that we carry with us throughout our lives. (Yes, I still love climbing a tree — there's one in Centennial Park that's now a designated reading spot!)

As we journey through life, we continue to learn and refine these recipes, adapting and evolving based on our interests and experiences. Our bodies, like sponges, absorb a wealth of information, while our brains act as the ultimate processor, orchestrating the intricate stages of movement.

Consider the stories of Ann and Bec, two individuals whose movements unintentionally reveal their past experiences as dancers. Ann's graceful arm extension beckons back to her childhood days of dance, while Bec's instinctual reliance on her pirouette leg reveals the deep imprint left by years of training.

Our bodies are remarkable adaptation machines, capable of both positive and negative adjustments based on the stimuli we provide. Just as repetitive movements can ingrain patterns into our muscles and joints, varied and dynamic activities can expand our movement vocabulary and enhance our overall physical literacy.

Take, for example, the inspiring journey of Jimmy, an 88-year-old dynamo whose dedication to learning and movement knows no bounds. From his early days on the soccer field to his current pursuits in table tennis with the family, Jimmy embodies the spirit of lifelong learning and continuous striving.

My favourite quote is from George Bernard Shaw: "We don't stop playing because we grow old; we grow old because we stop playing."

Embrace the joy of movement, and let it guide you on a lifelong journey of learning, growth and discovery. The benefits aren't just limited to the physical — the mental advantages may surprise you too.

So I invite you to ponder: how can you continue to play? What new movements, activities or challenges do you want to explore?

Until next week, stay Sassy,
Nikki
