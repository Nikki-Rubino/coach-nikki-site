Hey,

Don't assume anything — it just makes an ass out of u and me!

In today's society, a lot of pressure is placed on how we look. Who actually determines what "looks good" anyway? Everyone is different — body types, genetics, skeletal structures, personalities and goals. We're made to think that drastic weight loss is a great thing, and don't get me wrong, in some cases if that's what we've been working for, then good on you. But sometimes it can be a result of something much different.

A while back I stumbled across a post similar to this. You know when you're in a gym routine, you start to recognise people on the same schedule. This article described two ladies who'd do the usual hello head-nod. One hadn't been seen for a while, and once she returned she was a much smaller version of herself. Lady one: "Hello, I haven't seen you in ages, you're half your size — what's your secret, I want in on that?" Lady two: "My husband left me and I haven't slept well or barely eaten in three weeks."

At first I thought this might be fabricated for blog purposes, until I was part of a similar situation. Before a class, a couple of us were chatting. Lady one: "My gosh, you look incredible, look how skinny you are, how I wish I could lose that much weight — what have you been doing?" Lady two: "My dad died. I haven't had the appetite to eat anything while arranging funeral plans." We all stood there bewildered as to what to say next.

The moral of the story: if you don't know the whole story, please consider your choice of words. I was put in a similar situation last week, returning to a school where I'd taught boxing. The teacher looked completely different — new hair, no glasses, and had significantly lost weight. I only half knew her story. Instead of purely focusing on her appearance, I simply said, "Wow, didn't recognise you without your glasses, and with a huge new smile — it's been a while, what's changed with you?" That gives her an outlet to tell me, or not. Her response left me so happy: "Oh, I took up your advice and started weight training with a trainer. I've never felt better. I'm in a routine and sticking to it." If someone has been working towards something and is proud of it, they'll tell you — they'll want to share it.

All I'm asking is to be mindful of a person's situation. Dramatic weight loss obviously means something in their life has changed, and that may not always be positive — they or someone in their family could be ill, or have passed away, or something could have dramatically increased their stress or affected their sleep. A simple rephrase, from "wow, you look amazing!" (focused purely on aesthetics they may or may not be striving for) to "what's changed with you?" (an open question that lets them tell their story). This works on the other end of the spectrum too — if someone has gained weight, something has changed for them as well.

Let's all just be mindful of how we talk to ourselves and each other. Let's not purely focus on how someone looks. If you don't know someone's story, watch the words you use around them and to them.

Until next time,
Nikki
