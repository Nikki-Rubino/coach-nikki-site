Hey,

I'm sure you've heard the saying from Tim Ferriss, "Win the morning, win your day" — if you haven't, well, you have now. But have you ever thought about what winning the morning looks like to you?

There's a lot of hype around the 4am club, the 5am club, the hustle-and-grind mentality. If that's your jam and it inspires and invigorates you — amazing, you've found what works for you. I'm here this week to say that sometimes that hustle and grind isn't for everyone, and if you need a slower start to your day, that is absolutely fine. I know I do.

It has taken many years and many trials to see what actually makes me feel like I've won the day. I've studied at 4am before an advertising day, I've trained at 5:30am, I've started work at 5:45am in a park. For me, if I go gung-ho into adrenaline mode, my little brain and productivity dwindle by midday. I need a slower start and sunshine (or at least a barefoot walk outside). The slower start lets me calm my already overactive mind and focus on what needs to be achieved that day.

What I do encourage is making something outside part of your morning routine — and by morning, simply before noon. Sunshine and fresh air are both free and such an undervalued tool when it comes to health and wellness. Here are five benefits to sunshine in the morning:

1. **Vitamin D synthesis.** Sunlight is a natural source of vitamin D, essential for strong bones, regulating calcium levels, and supporting a healthy immune system. If you can move in some morning sunshine, even better!

2. **Improved mood and mental health.** Sunlight triggers the release of serotonin, a neurotransmitter associated with mood regulation and feelings of wellbeing. Morning sunlight can help alleviate feelings of depression and anxiety. Anyone else think sunshine on your face is just like a big warm hug?

3. **Regulation of circadian rhythm.** Natural light in the morning helps regulate your body's internal clock. It signals to your brain that it's time to wake up and be alert, and a well-regulated rhythm can improve sleep quality and energy levels throughout the day.

4. **Enhanced focus and productivity.** Morning sunlight has been linked to increased alertness and cognitive performance. It helps suppress melatonin (the sleepiness hormone) and boosts your energy, leading to better focus.

5. **Boosted physical activity.** Spending time outside in the morning sun may encourage you to walk, jog or do an outdoor workout — contributing to improved cardiovascular health, increased stamina, or simply walking the dog. The more you make movement part of your routine, the more your body will crave it, even on the cloudy days.

So I set a weekly challenge: get some sunshine into your mornings if you don't already, and see how you feel. Let me know what makes your ideal morning.

Until next week, stay Sassy,
Nikki
