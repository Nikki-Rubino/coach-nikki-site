Hey,

So we now hopefully understand the importance of our sleep. We make rash, not-so-optimal decisions and lose focus and creativity without it!

In an ideal world, we'd be getting between six and eight hours of quality sleep in optimum conditions. Sometimes we may not be able to create these conditions alone, or realistically there may be nights where I'm looking at a screen for longer than desired. To end this sleep series, here are some tools that can help:

**Why We Sleep by Matthew Walker (book recommendation).** I listened to the audiobook around two years ago and it resonated so much. I'd done shift work, long days and nights in front of the computer, never really thinking about my sleeping conditions. The ramifications of poor sleep made me realise I needed to change.

**Blue light blockers.** Whether it's screen protectors with it built in, blue-light-blocker glasses, or installing third-party software such as f.lux to change your device's screen settings after a certain hour.

**Light alarm clocks.** Have you ever woken to the sun rising, instead of a blasting sound? Clocks like Lumie are designed to slowly introduce warm light into your room, gradually prepping you to wake up naturally.

**Optimal temperature.** Ideally around 18.3 degrees. In summer, keep the room cool by closing blinds during the day, running a fan or air-con, and taking a hot shower (which naturally regulates the body's cooling system). In cooler months, add a hot water bottle or warmer socks to keep extremities warm.

**Avoid caffeine and alcohol.** These stimulants mess with the body's natural ability to relax.

**Darken that room.** Take out all electronics, turn anything with a standby light off at the wall. If you need to sleep with your phone in the room, turn it screen-side down. Cover any external light coming through windows or under doors. Keep the room as dark as you can.

I'd love to know if this helped you get back into a sleep routine after the holiday break.
