Hey,

When was the last time you experienced the power of the universal language of kindness? Can you remember a time in your life that has stayed with you from another person's act of kindness? Can you recall the last time you made someone else smile from one?

There's negativity all around us in the media. Humans have what psychologists call "negativity bias" — we tend to pay more attention to bad-seeming information than good-seeming information. So when you're in the realm of wanting to keep people's attention, of course you'll throw the negative in their face. Look at the news, for example: in that 30 to 60 minutes, take a tally of the feel-good stories compared to the sad, fear-evoking or negative ones.

An act of kindness is a win-win. The giver gets a happiness boost because it increases serotonin and dopamine — the neurotransmitters that create the feeling of satisfaction. Kindness also increases your sense of connection to others and gives each of you a sense of belonging. The effects are long-lasting, and the language has no boundaries; you don't need to speak the same language to show an act of kindness.

Let me share a personal story. Four and a half years ago, on the serene shores of Koh Samui, I found myself among 40 Animal Flow instructors from around the world, all gathering for a seven-day mentorship. Despite our diverse backgrounds and language barriers, our shared love for movement united us.

On the fifth day, we embarked on a snorkelling adventure. Four boats of amazing humans set off into the middle of the crystal clear ocean. Sounds amazing, right? Who wouldn't want to be in that position. Hi — I'm Nikki, an Aussie crippled by thalassophobia, the intense fear of the vast ocean and big bodies of water. As my peers joyously plunged in, panic set in as I sat on the boat's edge watching my companions disappear beneath the surface, my body physically shaking and tears filling up my goggles.

In that moment, Clarke, a fellow instructor from Macau, sat by me. His presence was a lifeline, a reminder that I wasn't alone. He simply said, "You go, I go, we go together." With his encouragement, we took the plunge together. But once underwater, I lost contact with his hand and my uncontrollable panic resurfaced. My face emerged in panic, realising there was nothing to hold, that I was simply floating with a dark abyss right below me.

That's when Zelos, another instructor from Hong Kong, floated in. Despite our language barrier, his gestures spoke volumes — his friendly smile and outstretched arms came and squeezed my hands tight, a kind, calming presence. We couldn't understand a single word of each other, nor had we had any verbal conversations over the past five days, but as he gently guided me to where the amazing underwater sea life was a few metres away, there were a lot of "are you ok?" hand gestures and smiles.

This experience taught me a profound life lesson: kindness transcends all boundaries. It's a universal language, understood by all, regardless of words spoken. Kindness is a vibe that connects us, especially in moments of vulnerability.

So, as you go about your day, remember the power of kindness. Remember how isolating it can be to enter a new environment — a new job, a new classroom, a new gym, a social event. Let's embark on a daily journey to, in the wise words of Harry Styles, "treat people with kindness". The great news is it's easy to start straight away: sending an encouraging text, offering to make a tea or coffee, letting someone in front of you in traffic, or a simple morning-walk smile to anyone who passes. Let's all make a conscious effort to spread this universal language wherever we go. You in?

I'd love to know a small act of kindness that has resonated with you — something that stood out and you'll never forget.

Until next week, stay Sassy,
Nikki
