Hey,

Can anyone else relate to this? I can recite every single word from the Fresh Prince of Bel-Air intro (matching dance moves included, obviously), but then walk into a room and completely forget why I went in there. Seriously, how does that even work?!

This week, I've been noticing something fascinating in the studio. Watching people move is like reading a story, even if I don't know their full history. Whether it's the way someone instinctively squats, flows through a stretch, or attempts to balance, their past movements leave little breadcrumbs.

Here's a fun example: I asked someone the other day, "How long did you do gymnastics for?" Their answer? "Oh, I haven't since I was about five!" But you could see it — every time she was proud of her efforts she'd celebrate with the arms in a Y, puffed chest, booty out and straight legs: the gymnastics "present" pose. For dancers, it's poise in their hands or feet. Soccer players? Their footwork gives them away every time.

That's the magic of muscle memory. As kids, we're constantly in motion — climbing, jumping, running, dancing (or in my case, attempting to climb trees and often failing spectacularly, or asking mum to come and save me). All those repetitive movements create a foundation of coordination and confidence that sticks with us, even decades later.

But here's the thing: as we age, if we stop practising certain movements or don't switch up our routines, we lose access to some of that memory. It's like an old dance floor — it needs a bit of dusting off before it's ready for action.

The good news? You can teach your muscles new memories at any age. Whether it's learning something new like Animal Flow (👋 my favourite tool to uncover where you feel confident and where you don't), revisiting a childhood hobby, or simply practising movements that support strength and mobility, your body is always ready to adapt. When you tap into that, not only do you move better, but you feel more connected to your body and your confidence soars.

So, what movement will you rediscover this week? Maybe it's a dance move from your younger days, a sport you haven't played in years, or a simple stretch that makes your body say "ahhh." Whatever it is, give those muscles a chance to remember how amazing you are.

Keep moving and stay Sassy,
Nikki
