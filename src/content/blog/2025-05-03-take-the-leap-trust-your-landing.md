Hey,

Last week, I did something I hadn't done in over four years: I went back to bouldering. (Think indoor rock climbing... but without the harness!)

I expected to feel a little rusty. I even expected to feel nervous at the top of the wall. But what caught me off guard? It wasn't the climb up — it was the *jump down*. Standing at the top, looking down at the soft mats, my brain went: "Uhh... you sure about this?"

It was eye-opening. I wasn't scared of the climb itself. I trusted my strength and technique. But letting go? Trusting the landing? That part felt unfamiliar — even a little scary.

It was a good reminder: sometimes it's not the *doing* that's hard. It's the *trusting yourself to land* that's the scary part.

And it's the same when you're getting back into anything you love — movement, hobbies, friendships, goals. Your body might be ready, your skills might be there... but your brain might need a minute to catch up. That's normal. It doesn't mean you've lost it. It just means you're human.

So if you're sitting on the edge of something you *want* to return to, but you're feeling weird about it — take the jump when you're ready. Sometimes, you just have to practise trusting the landing again.

What's something you've been thinking about getting back into? I'd love to hear and cheer you on!

Keep moving and stay Sassy,
Nikki
