Hey,

So we've set the goal, why we want to achieve it, and how we're going to implement change. Now: who is going to keep you accountable? Who's keeping tabs to make sure the new habits are being completed?

How many times have you said you were going to do something for yourself, set time aside, then something pops up and it doesn't get done? Generally we're driven either by proving something to ourselves, or by accountability to another person. Which one are you?

**The individual.** You want to prove to yourself you can do it. You like to see progress, tick things off your list, a thriver. If this sounds like you, a calendar system or a "don't break the chain" approach may be a good place to start. Revisit your how — what's the best way for you to track your own progress?

- Mark a calendar with a dot or X each time it's done. Don't break the chain.
- Mark the event in your notebook or journal — find a place you look at multiple times a day.
- Break the steps down in a checklist app like Trello, kept to yourself or shared.

**You serve others.** Keeping a promise to yourself isn't enough drive, but if you say you're going to meet someone, or tell someone who's expecting a response, that's your driver. (Personally, I'm this variation — having people I'd told things to, and a deadline, really helped me get stuff done towards the back end of last year.) Revisit your how:

- Tell someone — a family member, friend, coach or colleague. Ideally someone you interact with regularly. The more people aware, the more you'll be asked "how are you going with X?"
- Meet someone — to do the task together, or for a weekly or daily check-in. Reporting to another person keeps it front of mind.
- Use an app shared with another person. Trello is great for goal-setting and tick-listing; fitness apps and step trackers also record data you can share.

Maybe you're one of these types, or maybe you connect with both. Either way, create your accountability tool. How will you track this process? I'd love to hear what you choose or try.
