Hey,

My dad taught me how to use a spoon and ride a bike. What right did I have to help teach him how to walk again after a hip replacement?

I became a coach to give back — to break down complex movements into digestible chunks that people can understand and put into daily life straight away. Strength is confidence. Strength is independence. Strength and mobility are a means to quality longevity.

Let's talk about strength being confidence. When was the last time you had to think about an unknown environment? The majority of us don't really need to take into account what chair we'll be sitting on, whether there'll be stairs, or if the ground surface is even. These can seem like a non-event, right?

Let's take Nadia, for example — a very early client back in my mobile PT days. Nadia at the time was level 78 in life, and during that time had endured two left knee replacements and a left hip replacement. Nadia also has a big family, whose gatherings are usually based around large dinner tables, whether in a house or a restaurant. More and more, Nadia would try to avoid restaurants, but this also meant losing time with the family. Upon our first consult, we got to the nitty gritty of what was actually troubling her. Yes, there was a weakness in her legs, but for her that meant something else: she was unable to get up from the table without chair handles or the help of someone else, and her husband was becoming unable to always help in foreign surroundings out of the house.

Strength to Nadia is the confidence to sit with her family anywhere and know she has the tools to get herself back up.

I'd love to know: what is strength to you? Dig deep with whatever answer comes to you, and ask why that's important. Then repeat, again and again, until you really get to the root of your answer. You might be surprised what comes up when you dig a little deeper.
