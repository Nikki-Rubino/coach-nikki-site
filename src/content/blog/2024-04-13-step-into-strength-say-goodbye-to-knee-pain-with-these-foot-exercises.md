Hey,

Hands up if you've ever been told you have flat feet or need inner arch support. Now keep that hand up if you've experienced knee pain or discomfort. If you're nodding along, you're not alone — me too!

From a very young age, I was told I needed inner arch support. I was a fairly large teen and always plagued with knee pain and sore hips. Even though I lost weight, these niggles still plagued me. As an adult, I put it down to my sedentary TV editor job. It wasn't until I started to train barefoot that I could physically feel joints going in directions I couldn't control while training. It was really difficult for me to squat without my knee caving in or dumping pressure into my lower back.

My foot had been in the same type of padded cushion for over three decades! Asics Gel Kayanos were my shoes of choice ever since I could remember.

To put it into perspective: if you break a bone, it's in a cast usually for six to 12 weeks. The cast comes off and you can feel — and sometimes physically see — the muscle degeneration. Nothing in and around that joint has had any stimulus at all. Then there's the rehab process to gain strength back. So I ask: when, if at all, have you ever "trained your feet"? There are many muscles, ligaments and tendons that run all across, under and over our feet. Just like the rest of our body, they need stimulus.

Recently a few clients have been training on the Nabosu mat, or been introduced to the Nabosu ball — inviting the feet to a sensory experience to wake up the neural connection between feet and brain, and feeling the benefits.

This week I share four simple exercises to help wake up your arch. Aim to spend at least 60 seconds per exercise per foot — a simple eight minutes a day:

1. Release your foot
2. Foot tripod pull (pressure through big toe, little toe and heel)
3. Toe scrunches
4. Toe pulls

Let me know which one was the toughest for you to do.

Until next week, stay Sassy,
Nikki
