Hey,

Investment does not always mean dollars! Investments are also time, energy, thoughts and actions. So what do you actually value?

I never really thought about this until a random statement sparked it. Talking about health and fitness, the topic of where I train came up, and what I now pay weekly versus the $19 a week I previously paid at an aquatic centre. The response: "Is that like a celebrity gym or something? It just seems like a lot to pay for just a gym." There's no right or wrong here, just what we each value. To me it's an investment in myself — I respect the quality of the coaches, the ethics, the thought that goes into the programming. The coaches share knowledge bombs; I'm learning while I train. Yes, it sometimes takes 40 minutes to cut across peak-hour traffic, and yes, it costs more each week, but the value it gives me is priceless.

If you find the outcome valuable to you, you can find the time, energy, resources and cash. Some examples:

**Time** — I wanted to learn about topics that help me understand people, so I could serve my 1:1 clients better. Just switching to audiobooks instead of radio while driving racked up over 22 hours of listening and knowledge takeaways.

**Energy** — I say no to tasks that aren't on par with my values. I have video editing skills, but that skill takes a lot of time; I feel lucky I can now use it only for projects and people I choose to help. I can actually say no, and serve my energy into programming and creating.

**Cash** — it's amazing how the little things add up. Daily coffees, lunches, a snack here and there. What would happen if you switched a cafe coffee for an instant one, or brought your own tea bags to work? That's a minimum of $4 a day, $20 a week, $80 to $100 a month — from one less hot beverage a day.

**Resources** — I take pride in my inner circle. I admire the main people in my daily life and value their opinions. We all have different strengths and weaknesses; if a topic isn't my strong point, I ask someone I admire for recommendations, books, podcasts. People are usually willing to share knowledge if you're hanging with the right people.

So is it about time you actually start valuing yourself? How much time, energy or money do you invest in yourself? Do you spend time doing things that make your heart dance — a 10-minute chat with a friend, or 10 minutes of solace with a book and a cup of tea? When was the last time you put yourself first? I like to think of myself as a teapot — I can only give to others if I'm full myself.

Someone cut you off in traffic this morning — are you still reliving it angrily in the afternoon? A co-worker forever whinging — are you taking on their negativity? Are you giving your precious time to those who don't value it back? Life is short; shouldn't we spend it doing things that serve us and that we enjoy?

One more time: what do you actually value, and what are you investing in?

Until next time,
Nikki
