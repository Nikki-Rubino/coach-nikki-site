Hey,

The brief back story: I lost my older sister 13 years ago. It was sudden and unexpected — a moment in time where I can remember exactly where I was, and one I can't ever un-hear or un-see.

"You become the things you think about." — Earl Nightingale

She was my idol, and her influence helped me grow into the person I am today. We, like all sisters, had our moments of fights, banter and disappointments, but in the end we'd do anything for each other. I was overwhelmed with guilt for not doing enough to help her, until someone pointed out: "You did the best you could with the knowledge you had at the time." That helped me shed some new positive light on the situation.

As the years went by, it was the big events that were always hard — Christmas (our favourite holiday since we were kids), my wedding day, her birthday, my birthday (the last day I ever saw her alive). We all have a history, a story, a journey that has moulded us into where we are today. Life has its high of highs but also dark, soul-dwelling lows. Something as simple as a song or a smell can trigger something deep down. We are allowed to have an off day, to recognise these moments, and to tell others "I'm not ok."

First things first: I'm no psychologist, nor am I qualified professionally in this area. What I can offer is that I too have these dark, soul-crushing moments that take over when I least expect it, and I'm learning new things about myself and ways to deal with them.

June of 2019 was different. I was struggling — my usual tools of keeping busy and training hard simply weren't working. I was sad, lonely, with a blankness I couldn't shake. Sitting alone in the Tram Sheds focusing on client programming, a track came on my Spotify and left me a sobbing heap. A stranger walked past, saw this, and asked if I was ok. It was at that point I admitted: no, I'm not ok. She asked if I needed a hug, and I said yes please. There I was, in the arms of a stranger, sobbing — and I knew I needed some help to bring me out of this. I couldn't do it alone.

My takeaways over the years that have helped:

**1. Acknowledging the feeling.** Really assess what's bringing this emotion on. Is it a habitual reaction? An emotional time of year? A trigger — a song, a smell, a sighting? If it's something like that, acknowledge it and ride out what you need to. If the feeling lingers with no apparent cause, it may be worthwhile talking to a professional.

**2. Changing my state of mind with something that excites me.** What makes your heart dance? Talking to a certain someone, dancing around the room to your favourite track, watching cute puppies, changing your surroundings, getting into fresh air, hanging with your gym crew? Whatever it is, make it a priority. A shake-up is sometimes all it takes. Moving is my main outlet — I love a good roundhouse kick or punching combo on the bag, and my Animal Flow practice to keep my body moving in whatever way I have energy for.

**3. Tell someone "I'm not ok".** I'm grateful to have an amazing support network — old friends who know the emotional days, new friends seeing a mood shift, and my hubby who knows when I just need a big bear hug. After my hug from a stranger, I reached out to my top three girls to let them in on how I was feeling. A few simple words gave me the strength to get through the day. Not all of us are this lucky, so if you find yourself in this situation, know there are numbers you can reach out to.

So if you happen to notice a shift in someone — see a person sobbing, or notice unusual behaviour in a friend — sometimes a simple "Are you ok?" can be the nudge they need. If their words say one thing but their mood or body language says another, challenge them gently with "Are you sure?"

Two quotes to leave you with: "If there ever comes a day we can't be together, keep me in your heart and I'll stay there forever," and "You're braver than you believe, stronger than you seem, and smarter than you think." — Winnie the Pooh.

Hug a stranger if they need it. And if I'm classified as a stranger to you, don't be shy to reach out either.

Until next time,
Nikki
