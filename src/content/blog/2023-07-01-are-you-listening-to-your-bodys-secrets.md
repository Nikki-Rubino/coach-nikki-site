Hey,

It's time to tune in, pay attention, and unlock the secret signals your body sends your way. When was the last time you had a niggle and thought, "Ah, I'll be right, it's nothing"?

Our bodies do give us warning signs. Those niggles are the body's way of telling us one of three things:

- It's overused and needs some love or release. Think massage, either from a professional or self-release.
- It's weak. The area simply isn't strong enough for the demands you're putting it through.
- It's picking up the slack of something else not doing its full job.

This week there have definitely been some sore and sorry bodies in the gym from our JB Games, mine included. (For non-members, it's a social events day where all physical limits are tested with 12 movement events across four teams in the tribe.) Along with tribe members, a few PT client niggles are around, with shoulders and knees in the mix.

My own body had been feeling off since coming back from a month of travel and movement workshops. I sleep in a very random, not-so-good position for my spine, hips and neck too. Am I aware of it? Yes. Do I try to combat it every night? Yes. Can I control how I actually fall asleep? No. I'm aware when my right hip is hiked up, my lower back hurts, and my shoulders start bearing some of the brunt. I combat my sleeping position with movement in the opposite direction each morning. Is this enough? Nope. I need to see the chiro at least every three months (it used to be fortnightly in my TV editor days) to prime my back into a neutral position.

Understanding your own body is ideal. Knowing what's working for you or against you is key. Things to bear in mind when it comes to niggles: Is it a recurring thing? Have you done anything new or different in the last 36 to 48 hours that could have triggered it? Is it time to seek a professional — a coach, physio or chiro? What positions do you spend the majority of your time in?

Listen to the niggles and learn what they're trying to tell you, before they turn into a full-blown injury. And if you do happen to get an injury, use it as a learning curve and a way to give yourself a new focus. Think about the limbs that are still available to you. In 2020 I tore a calf — but gained a chin-up!

Until next week, stay Sassy,
Nikki
