Hey,

Have you ever considered how your mindset can shape your outcomes even before you've taken the first step to even trying?

Think about it: we often make assumptions about what we can or cannot do, even before we've tried. These assumptions are like mental barriers that hold us back from realising our potential. What if we challenged these assumptions and approached life with an open, growth-oriented mindset? "I may not have been able to execute this before, but what about now?"

Putting it into a movement perspective: perhaps you'd previously attempted a movement. It could be as simple as going up stairs on a non-dominant leg, a push-up, a chin-up, a deadlift, an Animal Flow sequence, or going down the stairs with control. Our bodies have their own story and movement memories. They adapt and evolve, and if you've been working towards a goal, you're building strength and awareness for your body to adapt.

This is why, as a coach, I set benchmarks or baselines of your current movement patterns. We work on building awareness, strength, motor control and mobility. Many times this week I've heard "I can't", or "if I do that, this hurts", or "I don't have the strength for that". My response: is that a fact, or an assumption? How about we simply try and see what happens this time around?

The excitement I see in people when a story gets re-written, and the realisation of "I can do it", is so damn rewarding. It could be as complex as a controlled push-up, or simply "I can walk down the stairs without knee pain or the rails". Every body is different, every body is capable of adapting to positive environments, and every body's story can have added chapters with a plot twist.

Don't get me wrong, I'm totally guilty of assuming things I thought I could never do — believing that because it didn't work out last time, why would it now? Next time you're questioning yourself, ask yourself two questions: Is this a true fact? What evidence do I have that supports that? More than likely, that little inner critic voice is playing games with you. Why not try to see the evidence for yourself first?

What can you attempt this week that can rewrite an assumption?

Until next week, stay Sassy,
Nikki
