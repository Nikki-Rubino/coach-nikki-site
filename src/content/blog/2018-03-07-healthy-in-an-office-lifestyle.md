Hey,

So you sit at a computer all day and think, how can I fit in the time to be healthy? Today I'm here to talk about fitness in the office — I've been there too. Before becoming a PT, I was a TV editor. Here are some simple life hacks to help you on your fitness journey.

**1. Be food prepared.** How many times a day are you caught out and just grabbing something on the go? A little time on weekends doing a cook-up works wonders. Cook a protein, a grain and some veggies, add good fats like nuts, EVOO or avocado, and you have ready ingredients at your disposal — chuck it all in a bowl for a quick dinner, or layer in a jar for a portable lunch. Make snacks in a batch and freeze them to have on hand.

**2. Move.** Have you ever sat on your hand long enough that it went numb? That's what happens to your glutes from sitting on them all day. Three simple tips I used in my office: drink from a smaller (500ml) water bottle so you have to get up to refill more often; set an alarm every hour to stand up and stretch (if we sit all day, our hip flexors and chest get tight); and instead of calling someone a few metres away, walk over to them, or make it a priority to walk up and down any nearby stairs at least four times throughout the day.

**3. Hydrate.** The majority of the time, we snack when we're thirsty — so get up and refill that water bottle. If you need help drinking the amount your body needs (approximately 30ml per kilo of bodyweight), use a smaller bottle, or grab a marker and divide a larger one into timed sections as a visual goal through the day. I like to add edible berries and citrus too — strawberry, lime and mint; cucumber, lime, mint and apple; or orange, blueberry and cucumber.

**4. Breathe.** No matter how busy and overwhelmed you are, everyone needs to breathe. A lot of us get bogged down in emails, to-do lists and deadlines, always scheduling time for everyone else. When do we schedule time for ourselves? Spend 60 seconds breathing with intention — ideally outside or somewhere quiet. Close your eyes, take a long slow breath in for four counts (filling your belly, not your ribcage), then exhale for the same. Repeat three to four times, then continue with what needs to be done.

**5. Laugh.** Laughing releases feel-good endorphins, makes us smile, and leaves a happy moment in time. Either laugh at yourself or make someone else laugh. I'm constantly getting out of my chair to do a little dance at the water cooler — one, to get me moving, and two, to amuse anyone walking up the stairs. Find a buddy to tell a lame joke, try a new dance move for a reaction, or watch your favourite genre on YouTube. Whatever makes you giggle, take two minutes to go and do it. Laughing awakens our core too — a win-win!

Go on and give these a try. Feel free to let me know what made you giggle today!

Live, dance, laugh,
Nikki
