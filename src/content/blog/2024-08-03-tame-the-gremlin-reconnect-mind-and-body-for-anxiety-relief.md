Hey,

Well, doesn't Sunday morning fly around fast.

This week, I want to talk about that pesky little gremlin (not the cute Gizmo kind, but the kind that gets wet after midnight) that sneaks up on us when we least expect it — anxiety.

I've noticed a common theme this week among my ninjas, and definitely in myself. If you're someone who lives in your head, don't underestimate the magic of getting back into your body.

Life can get a little chaotic at times, filled with "what ifs" and worst-case scenarios (or is that just me?). Living in this state of mind can be exhausting and overwhelming, and for some, a daily occurrence. (Inside Out 2 does an amazing job of depicting this feeling.)

This little gremlin often pops up when we're trying to figure out something far into the unknown future. Our thoughts whirl around, jumping from one thing to another, and our bodies can't keep up. Have you ever gotten injured while thinking about something else? Anxious thoughts can make you feel disconnected from your body.

Our physical body is the one thing we can control at any given time. Returning to your body can help prevent injuries or accidents and give you back a sense of physical control. So how can we regain it?

**Breathe and move.** Take a moment to breathe and ground yourself. Apps like Headspace and Calm offer guided meditations. Step outside into fresh air, change the texture under your feet, fill your lungs, and take long, controlled walking strides to see if you can stay balanced while doing so.

**When in doubt, scribble it out.** Write down your thoughts and feelings — on a scrap of paper or in your phone. Getting those worries out of your head and onto paper helps you see them from a different perspective. Then assess what you can tackle first.

**Gratitude.** Each day, write down three things you're grateful for. It sounds simple, but it works wonders. It shifts your focus from what's going wrong to what's going right.

**Shake it off.** Find something that gets you out of your head and into your body — for me that's my Animal Flow practice. For you it might be yoga, Pilates, rock climbing, swimming, running, or dancing — whatever makes your heart dance and your body move.

These are surface-level redirections — I'm all for seeking help, advice, or a simple listening ear, whether from family, a friend, or a professional. Every step you take to manage anxiety is a victory. Celebrate those small wins. Remember, we're all in this together, and it's okay to ask for help when you need it.

Take care and stay Sassy,
Nikki
