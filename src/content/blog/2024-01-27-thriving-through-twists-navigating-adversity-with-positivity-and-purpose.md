Let's dive into a common thread we all navigate: adversity. Life often resembles a rollercoaster, complete with unexpected loops and turns. As we transition back to our regular routines — post-Australia Day celebrations, back to work, and kids returning to or starting school — the holiday season officially concludes. However, the impact of the holidays varies for each person. For some it's a time of joy spent with loved ones; for others it may have felt challenging. Businesses may have thrived, while some struggled to make ends meet. It's essential to recognise that everyone has their own unique story, and we can't fully comprehend the experiences of others.

In the midst of these transitions, my guiding principle is simple: kindness. Not everyone has the capacity to handle additional negativity. By practising kindness, we create space for understanding and compassion.

But what if you're a person who struggled over the holiday period, and you feel too overwhelmed and low to go about your day? Fear not — you've got some tools up your sleeve.

1. **Find the silver lining.** Life's challenges often come with hidden opportunities. Maybe that unexpected detour is a chance to learn something new or discover a strength you didn't know you had. I've mentioned before the story of tearing a calf and gaining a chin-up. Currently, having to upheave my veggies from the patch and transport them into pots, I'm learning how to care for them all over again.

2. **The power of gratitude.** Take a moment each day to count your blessings — a good cup of coffee, a sunny day, even a friendly smile from a stranger. Gratitude is a powerful tool to shift your focus from what's going wrong to what's going right. Having something that prompts you to write your gratitude down is so powerful, or simply going around a table and everyone saying one thing out loud. Ever bought a new car, then happened to see your same model everywhere on the road?

3. **Dance it out.** When life gets tough, put on your favourite song and dance like nobody's watching. Movement and music are magical mood lifters. The levels of chemicals in the brain — serotonin, stress hormones, endorphins — change when you exercise or simply move. I dare you to put on your favourite track, dance, and still be in an angry or down mood. Is it possible?

4. **Connect with your tribe.** Share your struggles with friends or family. Sometimes a listening ear or a supportive hug is all you need. Your tribe are the ones who notice the subtle changes in you. These are the people you can offload to, vent to, laugh with — not necessarily looking for answers, but creating a safe space. (And remember, not everyone will have the bandwidth to take on your conflicts, so be sure it's the right time and place.)

5. **Build consistent habits.** Healthy habits are the superheroes of resilience. Whether it's a morning jog, meditation, or a nightly reading ritual, stick to routines that nourish your mind and body. Once the habit is built, you can take it into any environmental change that happens.

6. **Learn from setbacks.** Life's lessons are often disguised as setbacks. Instead of dwelling on failure, treat it as a lesson. What can you learn? How can you grow?

Remember, you're stronger and more resilient than you think. Life's challenges are just opportunities in disguise. So put on your cape, embrace the adventure, and conquer the rollercoaster of life! Wishing you a week filled with positivity, growth, and a sprinkle of unexpected joy.
