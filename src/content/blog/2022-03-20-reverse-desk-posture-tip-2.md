Hey,

Hand up if you feel any pain or discomfort in your lower back when trying to lift or reach for anything overhead. Now if your hand is up, spot-check — is that ribcage flared out and your back arched to get it up there?

If your day consists heavily of desk work, driving, sitting — anything where you're in a forward, rounded posture — your thoracic spine (the vertebrae that run along your ribcage) can curve forward. If it only knows this rounded posture, then anytime you try to stand tall, reach overhead and keep your ribs down, your body actually can't. So to compensate, it flares out the ribcage and dumps pressure into the lower back, or restricts you from reaching overhead at all.

Think of movement environments as yin and yang — your body adapts to the environment it's subjected to on a regular basis. Desk positions are rounded, so you need to let the spine feel extension, or a lean-back sensation if you will. I encourage you to try this foam roller drill as a start to help combat rounded posture, open up the front of the body, and let the body experience spine extension.

Aim for two to three rounds of 10 reps with a one-to-three-minute hold, keeping your elbows tucked in. Let me know how it feels for you.
