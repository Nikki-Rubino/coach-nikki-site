"The key is not to prioritise what's on your schedule, but to schedule your priorities." — Stephen Covey

So you've had a week to think about what you really want to achieve, and why it's important to you. Now what? Let's look at it all objectively.

Following the steps from James Clear's *Atomic Habits*, there are four laws to creating a new habit, which then becomes part of your routine. A four-step loop underlies all human behaviour: cue, craving, response and reward. With repetition, this neurological feedback loop leads to the cultivation of new habits (and that feedback can serve a positive or a negative response — today we're focusing on the positive).

If we want to change something for the positive, think of these factors:

1. **Cue:** make it obvious.
2. **Craving:** make it attractive.
3. **Response:** make it easy.
4. **Reward:** make it satisfying.

Knowing what you want to achieve is the first step; now, how can this formula work for you? I'll go first. Mine is a movement skill, so I need 10 to 30 minutes of dedicated practice. My goal: reach a 10-second freestanding handstand. I know I'm motivated by inspiring and encouraging others.

- **Cue:** I'm lucky enough to work in an environment that feeds this goal, but I tend to do admin upstairs off the main gym floor. So I'll set aside time to finish admin downstairs on the gym floor.
- **Craving:** I love to chill in the sunshine or on the deck at Jungle Brothers. This is the desired place to practise handstands — hardwood floor and plain black walls.
- **Response:** if I'm already doing admin downstairs, I don't have to move anywhere.
- **Reward:** I get vitamin D, skill practice, and as I work three nights a week here, members will see my progress and potentially get inspired to hit their own movement goals.

Now, what can you do to make the same four laws work for you? Feel free to share — I love hearing everyone's ideas.
