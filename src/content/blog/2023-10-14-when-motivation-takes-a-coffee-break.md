Hey,

Happy Sunday!

This week I share that I had to tell my brain to shut up, and simply let the body get the job done.

You wanna know a secret? Motivation won't always be available. Motivation is a fickle friend — it shows up unannounced and leaves when you need it most. But discipline? Discipline is the reliable partner that's always there to pick you up, even on the toughest days. It's what keeps you going when motivation picks up and leaves. Partner this with accountability and you have one powerful dynamic duo.

Let's set the scene. It was Wednesday. I had finished a full morning of client-facing sessions — three virtual and two in the gym. It was a lovely sunny day, my brain was tired and yelling at me to just go home, head for a beach walk, train tomorrow. My, oh my, that was one tempting and okay-sounding plan. That pesky motivation that wrote "two chin-ups for Christmas" on the goals board was nowhere in the room. Discipline was peeking around the corner, trying to negotiate, saying we have the time to bust it out now and maybe still get in a beach walk — but it was not overly winning the mental battle. Then accountability busted through the door! My coach (yes, coaches have coaches — I'm only human too) reached out: "Reminder to film your sets this week so I can see where you're at." Blargh. Ok, there it was. I knew I would not have the time to film everything if I trained "tomorrow".

Every ounce of my brain was not happy with this situation. The ideal environment was not set — I hate training alone, there were no vibes to bounce off. Alone with my brain and body in an empty gym to get it done. Ok, pump-up time, let's try music, my go-to… it wasn't working! First set done, my body actually felt strong and capable. I knew at that moment I simply had to tell my brain to shut up. I narrated my inner voice to talk to my body only — be my body's cheerleader, pretend that my brain was like a spoilt child throwing a tantrum, leave it be and walk away, I'll deal with you later.

In hindsight, it turned out to physically be an okay session. I got to feel how far I'd come since week one of this program phase.

Remember, success is not born from a single burst of motivation; it's the result of consistent effort and discipline. The three pillars:

**Habit.** Consistency is key. By making your training a non-negotiable part of your routine, you turn it into a habit. It's like brushing your teeth — you wouldn't skip it because you didn't "feel like it", right?

**Discipline.** Doing what needs to be done, even when you don't feel like it. It's about honouring your commitments to yourself. When you cultivate discipline, your journey becomes less about "feeling like it" and more about getting the job done for the bigger picture.

**Accountability.** Share your goals and progress with a friend, a coach, or your workout buddy. Accountability partners make sure you don't slip through the cracks, especially on those days when your brain is in full rebellion mode.

As you go through this week, keep in mind that discipline and determination are your superpowers. They'll carry you further than fleeting motivation ever could. So when your brain tries to sabotage your progress, tell it to hush and let your body take the lead. Keep pushing, keep growing, and keep believing in yourself. You are capable of amazing things.

I'd love to hear your own "I did not feel like ___" story — your moments of triumph over that sneaky inner voice.

Until next week, stay Sassy,
Nikki
