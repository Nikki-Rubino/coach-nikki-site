Hey,

Back into the routine, or still on holidays? Woohoo for play days!

Who else finds it a breeze to pledge to start something new during a break? "I'll cook fresh every night, walk daily, try a new class, read a book!" Sound familiar? Guilty as charged — I commit to holiday-inspired resolutions, only to watch them fade when routine takes over. The struggle is real, right?

So how do we make those well-intended habits stick? Say hello to "habit stacking." It's about piggybacking on existing routines. Take, for example, those standing drills to improve ankle mobility — do them while brushing your teeth. Twice a day you'll brush, and the mobility work gets done. Short-term reward, check!

New Year's resolutions often include pledges like "I'll start training multiple times a week." Admirable, but the how, when and where can be stumbling blocks. Enter the power of walking — two birds, one stone: making time to move and creating a new movement habit. Initially, a 60-minute daily walk may seem overwhelming. Where's the time, what to do, and the cost? Valid concerns.

To make it stick, we need to build the habit, remembering that motivation fluctuates. Discipline is the true champion. Take an hour of walking as an example. An adult may need 60 to 90 minutes for 10K steps — a daunting task. But what if you break it into three 20-minute walks? Morning, lunch and evening: set a timer, walk 10 minutes, turn around, and head back. Keep it as long or short as suits you. The key is establishing a habit of moving at a specific time. Substitute walking with something you enjoy. Once the habit forms, your body will crave it. On a busy day, if walking isn't feasible, you've still ingrained a movement habit at that time.

Start small, establish habits aligned with your goals, and let's go!
