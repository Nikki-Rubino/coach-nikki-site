> "It only takes a second to call a girl fat, and she'll take a lifetime trying to starve herself. Think before you act." — Harry Styles

This week I want to share how an impromptu ticket purchase to Harry Styles gave me a body image reality check.

My TikTok feed has been swamped with Harry Styles clips over the last few months. In them, the energy in the room is so positive and energetic — it really seems like a fun space to be in. So after a trying week, when my own energy levels were low, I bought a last-minute ticket. I honestly thought it would be sold out, but I tried my luck anyway. Two mates would be on the ground, which meant me in a seated area alone. Out of my comfort zone — challenge accepted.

**So next up was an outfit.** Time to restock something bright and comfy. But it's been about six years since I bought clothes that weren't activewear. Honestly, I barely go anywhere without being in stretchy clothing or jeans.

All the bright pants came with me into the changing room. The last time I bought pants I'd always been a size 10–12, and that's what I am in activewear.

**At first it was horrifying — nothing was fitting right.** The ex-fat-kid thoughts came back louder than ever. On my first day of high school I needed a size 22 shirt. Weight, numbers, and clothes have always been slightly triggering for me. I had to stop, take a moment, and assess. I'm not the unhealthiest I've ever been. The parts of my body where the fabric wasn't sitting right are the parts I work damn hard. These thighs are chunky but damn strong. This belly fluctuates day by day. And I've always had a longer-than-average torso.

Most of all, I was so out of the game that I was shopping in the shops I went to as a teen — but this vessel is different to my 20s, as it should be. I toddled off into what I'd always labelled the "adults' clothing store", then realised: um, hello, you're 39, get your bum in there for a look.

The moral of the story: it's ok to buy a bigger size and find clothes that make you feel good — not ones you have to manipulate yourself for. Choose fabrics you like, wear colours you want, and dress for your shape and comfort.

I came out with an outfit in size 14. Does it feel good on my skin? Yes. Is it comfy? Yes. Do I feel good in it? Yes.

Numbers are purely that — numbers. Don't beat yourself up if clothes don't fit like they did decades ago. If there are genuine health concerns with your current body shape, that's of course different, and lifestyle habits would need amending. But otherwise, don't stress if you size up over time — especially if you're training and gaining muscle.

Wish me luck at a concert by myself. At least I'll be in comfy pants.
