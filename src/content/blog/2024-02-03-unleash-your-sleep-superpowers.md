Hey,

A good night's sleep is the ultimate productivity hack. Sleep is how the body literally recharges — but how full is your battery each morning?

Think about how many good decisions were made the last time you were tired. Were you feeling calm and able to make rational choices through the day around food and drink? Did you have energy for the tasks at hand? Did you have the cognitive ability to speak fluently? Brain fog is a real deal, especially in women, when quality sleep is not received.

Full disclosure: many things can disrupt our sleep, some we can change or improve. But there are two main things we maybe can't control — shift work, and a newborn baby. These will definitely hinder the quality of sleep and are a whole other area to touch on. So if this is you, take what info resonates at this point in time.

**Set yourself up for sleep success!**

**Have a downtime routine.** Create an atmosphere that tells your brain it's time to wind down. Turn off your screens, take a shower, do gentle stretch and mobility drills, journal, meditate, do breathwork, read, colour, create art, or simply dim the lights and listen to chill music. (I understand that anyone with an overactive thinking brain or a hectic, stimulated job may need simple trash TV to wind down and bring the adrenaline down. In that case, try investing in blue-light-blocking glasses.) Take five minutes to brain dump the day — write down anything that comes to mind. It doesn't have to make sense; simply get it out of your head and onto paper.

**Create an optimal sleeping space.** Ideal sleeping spaces are dark, around 18 degrees, and quiet — a sanctuary for the brain to delve into deep REM (rapid eye movement) sleep, the zone where the body can fully relax and recharge. As someone who has recently moved and is currently without blinds, flyscreens or fans, this has been an adjustment. Some tools to combat the situation: an eye mask (I was always reluctant, but after a few nights the amount of light entering the room was wreaking havoc), and Loop Quiet earplugs — honestly a game changer. They drown out the hum of the aircon, reduce outside noise, but still let me hear the deep sleep tunes playing, and they're comfortable.

**Avoid stimulants.** Such as caffeine after midday, high-intensity exercise more than two hours out, and alcohol. If you do love a warm beverage as part of your downtime, choose a herbal or non-caffeinated variety.

Upgrading your sleep has multiple benefits for health, wellness, cognitive function and the body's ability to repair itself. Did you know that a poor night's sleep can affect learning the following day by up to 40%?

What can you do tonight to set yourself up for sleep success?
