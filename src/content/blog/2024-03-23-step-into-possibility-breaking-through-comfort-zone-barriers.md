Hey,

Do you ever find yourself feeling stuck in a rut, hesitant to venture beyond the familiar? It's all too easy to let fear and doubt hold us back from embracing new experiences and opportunities for growth. But what if I told you that stepping out of your comfort zone could be the key to unlocking a world of possibilities?

Picture this: you're feeling apprehensive about trying something new, worried about the potential for failure or injury. These doubts linger, casting a shadow over your confidence. Sound familiar? I get it — as someone who totally changed careers at 33, from the safety net of a TV editor in advertising to fitness coach, I was petrified and plagued with self-doubt. Seven years later I still am! But once you've done it, you can use that as actual data to tell yourself you were okay, you learned something, and you're capable of whatever comes up next time.

Let's break free from this cycle of negativity and self-doubt. Here are three crucial steps to help you overcome the barriers holding you back.

**Acknowledge your fears.** It's okay to feel afraid of the unknown. But instead of letting fear paralyse you, acknowledge it as a natural part of the process. As you may be aware, I'm absolutely terrified of the ocean — or any large body of water where I can't see the bottom. At 40, I'm still unsure whether I've always had thalassophobia or whether it started when I watched *Jaws* at the age of seven. Now that I live near the ocean, I've been slowly chipping away at getting myself deeper on and in the water, piece by piece — starting with buying reef shoes and a paddle board, and inviting whoever I can to come near the water with me. Remember, you have a support network; you don't have to do it alone.

**Shift your perspective.** Rather than viewing challenges as obstacles, reframe them as opportunities for learning and self-discovery. Embrace the mindset of a lifelong learner, eager to explore new horizons. (Again, some of you know that in high school a teacher told me it was "unprofessional" to talk using my hands and made me do a speech with them behind my back — for the record, I couldn't. Since then I've been so self-conscious about speaking in any professional setting. Last year I joined a six-month speaker academy course, and it's now time for the final recorded submission.)

**Take action.** Now that you've identified your fears and shifted your perspective, it's time to take the plunge. Start small, setting achievable goals that gradually stretch your limits. Whether it's trying a new hobby, joining a fitness class, picking up a heavier weight, striking up a conversation with a stranger, catching a plane, or jumping into the ocean — each step forward brings you closer to your full potential. Everyone is different; what scares you may be someone else's strength. Who can you turn to for advice, help or guidance?

Take the first step today by committing to one small act of courage. Trust yourself, and remember that the only limits that exist are the ones we impose upon ourselves. Every day I pinch myself at who I get to interact with each week, where I get to work from, and how much happier day-to-day life is.

Go forth and conquer! Let me know how you go.

Until next week, stay Sassy,
Nikki
