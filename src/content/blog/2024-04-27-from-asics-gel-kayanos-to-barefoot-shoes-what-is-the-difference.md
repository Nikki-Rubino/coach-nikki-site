Hey,

So we've been discussing foot health, mobility and strength. I'd love to know if you tried the drills over the last two weeks and what you noticed.

Being in a cushioned shoe — or, as I like to call them, foot coffins — means our feet don't get exposed to any sensory receptors. What does this mean? Our feet can't navigate the terrain underneath them. This can cause issues with balance, proprioception, foot strength and toe mobility. Why is this important? It can eventually affect our knees, hips and spine.

So how is a barefoot shoe different?

**1. Flexible sole.** The shoe can bend easily, so your foot can actually do some work. Try this: put your current shoes on and try to do a calf raise, then try again with your shoes off. Feel a difference?

**2. Roomy toe box.** The toes can actually spread out naturally. Have you ever experienced blisters on your pinkie toe, or a bunion? Now check where the pain point is and where that spot sits in your shoe. My own pinkie toe used to be one big blister — forever being crushed, it eventually started taking refuge hiding under my fourth toe! Try this: trace around your whole foot while standing on it. Now take your usual everyday shoe, remove the innersole, and place it on your tracing. Does it match? Now stand on your innersole barefoot. What do you see?

**3. No arch support.** This actually improves foot health over time. There are 29 muscles associated with the human foot: 10 originate outside the foot but cross the ankle joint to act on it, and 19 are intrinsic foot muscles. I get it — I too was told from a young age that I needed arch support. But imagine living with a leg or arm always in a cast, unable to move to its full potential, simply stuck encased in a hard shell. Gloves are shaped like hands. Pants are shaped like legs. Shoes are shaped like…?

**4. Zero drop sole.** The sole is the same thickness throughout, promoting natural posture. Think it's only women's high heels that have a heel? Take a look at yours closely.

There are many brands of barefoot shoes now that the word is getting out, and even more exciting transitional brands that keep some of the cushion you're accustomed to while adding the other three benefits — roomy toe box, zero drop sole and flexibility. Transitional brands include Skora and Altra; minimal-sole brands include Vivo and Xero.

A little game to practise: try the "Baywatch walk" — walk as slowly and in as controlled a way as you can. Can you keep control of heel first, then toes? Can you push off your back big toe? Can you coordinate opposite arm and leg?

Happy to give any further insight about barefoot walking and shoes — simply reach out by replying. I'd love to know what resonated with you this month.
