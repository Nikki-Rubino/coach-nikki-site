Hi Friend,

R U OK?Day is the national day of action reminding Australians that every day is a day to ask "are you ok?" and start a meaningful conversation whenever they spot the signs that someone they care about might be struggling.

Today I want to shed light on two things: how to start that conversation, and some resources if they respond "no". I want to preface this — I'm not a psychologist of any kind. This topic is close to my heart; my big sister went through major mental shifts and diagnoses, and looking back I just wasn't equipped to handle it. A mentor helped me by saying, "You did all you could with the tools you had at the time." That led me to want more tools, so in 2020 I took part in Mental Health First Aid run by the Rise Foundation Australia.

Here's the formula I learned — **S.A.F.E.**

**1. Speak up if you see changes.** If something seems different — their mood, appearance, behaviour — speak up. Pick a quiet, neutral place. Make sure there's time to talk (not as they're walking out the door). Approach from concern, not judgment. Keep it comfortable, like any other conversation, and have emotional and practical support on hand.

**2. Ask open questions.** Use language that comes from a place of comfort. Ask clarifying questions and give them a chance to tell their side. Watch their body language — "I'm fine" while looking at the ground is a red flag. Reassure them of confidentiality. And let's make asking about suicide less taboo — practise saying it out loud, even in the mirror.

**3. Factual responses.** We don't have all the answers, so let's not pretend we do. Don't lie or pretend to know how they feel. Use your relationship and your story — tell them what you've noticed and that you're concerned. Two ears, one mouth: listen intently. And don't over-promise; offer what's sustainable for you both.

**4. Encourage help that works.** Professional, medical, lifestyle. Ask what's worked in the past — this may not be the first time. We can't force it; they may not be ready. Check back in and make it an ongoing conversation. If they're resistant, gently explore why.

**How to calmly ask, from a place of concern:** "Hey mate, what's happening… I've noticed you haven't been yourself… what's going on? Do you need anything?" Simple, open-ended questions let them respond as much or as little as they wish.

**Resources**

National 24-hour crisis services:

- **Lifeline:** 13 11 14 — text 0477 13 11 14
- **Beyond Blue:** 1300 22 4636
- **Suicide Call Back Service:** 1300 659 467
- **MensLine:** 1300 78 99 78
- **Kids Helpline:** 1800 55 1800

Information and support: beyondblue.org.au, lifeline.org.au, reachout.com, blackdoginstitute.org.au, ruok.org.au, headspace.com, sane.org.

Mental health matters. I hope this helps you feel more confident asking the question — or finding support if you're having a rough time yourself. Sometimes it can feel like you've been buried, but what if you've merely been planted?

Let's start a conversation today, and every day.

Nikki

*This is a sensitive topic. If you're struggling yourself, please reach out to one of the services above, or someone you trust — support is available.*
