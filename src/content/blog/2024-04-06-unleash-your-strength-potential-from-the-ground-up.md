Hey,

Ever noticed how much attention we pay to our feet? Well, if you've ever been coached by me, you'll know I'm always looking at your feet. Why? Because they're the foundation of our bodies — our roots in the ground, so to speak. They adapt to the environment, the surfaces we walk on, and the stimulus they receive.

When was the last time you walked barefoot? If it's been a while, maybe it's time to kick off those shoes and reconnect with the ground beneath you. And if you're already a barefoot aficionado, kudos to you! But here's a question: how many different surfaces are you exposing your feet to?

Think about it. How does your body feel after walking barefoot? Do you experience pain in your heels, arches, knees, hips, lower back, toes or ankles? Ever noticed soreness in those areas after a day at the beach, or a stroll in shoes that aren't your everyday kicks?

Are you one of those folks in heavily padded shoes or with prescribed orthotics? I used to be in that camp too, with constant hip and lower back pain in my 20s — until I discovered the wonders of barefoot training.

Seven years ago, I stumbled upon barefoot training at Jungle Brothers (yes, where I'm now a coach) and through my Animal Flow community. It was a game-changer. Strengthening my feet, just like any other muscle in my body, helped unleash a whole new level of strength further up the chain.

It wasn't easy at first. Training barefoot felt weird and clunky. But over time, I learned how to strengthen my feet and improve my overall posture. And now, I'm on a mission to share my journey with you.

This month, I'll be sharing my story of unleashing strength potential from the ground up. Let's learn together what our feet are trying to tell us and how we can empower ourselves through barefoot training.

One of the best things I love to do to start my day is to walk on different surfaces — carpet, hardwood floor, outdoor balcony tiles, concrete, sand and rocks. A barefoot walk is a staple in my world now. Have you tried it?

Stay grounded!

Until next week, stay Sassy,
Nikki
