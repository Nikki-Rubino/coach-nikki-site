Hey,

Do you ever feel like life is on auto-pilot sometimes? There's a routine that feels safe, comfortable and certain. But is that safe, comfy routine keeping you from exploring what you can really achieve? You may have heard the saying, "Smooth sailing never built a skilled sailor."

At times we can get so into the same routine that when something shakes it up, the world seems upside down. Our minds fear change and uncertainty; they'll try anything to keep you feeling safe. (For example, you leave work, drive home, and end up in your driveway without really paying attention to how you got there.) When things become less predictable and feel out of our control, we experience a strong state of threat. This can decrease motivation, focus, self-control and overall wellbeing, and even impair your working memory — you can't hold as many ideas in mind to solve problems. Threats of uncertainty literally make us less capable, because dealing with them just isn't something our brains evolved to do.

I had such an experience this week. I'm still looking for a place to rent, with a deadline looming. There's uncertainty about where I'll live, what that means for my business, and a work schedule end-of-an-era that's been constant for two years. If I'm honest, life feels like a magic trick where the tablecloth gets ripped out and the fine china will either survive or smash. I had one simple decision — what juice did I feel like on my walk — and my brain freaked out, an internal meltdown, and I simply walked away and made no decision at all. I'll just stick with my water bottle!

On my walk, I thought about how crazy that was. After some research, I realised what was going on and gave myself a stern talking-to. A day later I took a day trip to Shellharbour alone to support a friend's breathwork event. It was an amazing day — flying solo on a road trip, supporting friends, getting out of my comfort zone. The little reset I needed. On the way home, a road was closed. I'd set it in my mind that I'd fill up petrol at one intersection I knew, but with the detour I never made it there. Here I was, alone at night on a different highway, knowing I had 20km more to get home than my petrol tank had. I started laughing that the day prior I couldn't decide on a damn juice. But today my brain had had a shake-up. A different route, new people, new scenery — and my brain went into problem-solving mode on its own, doing the kilometre maths and backup plans B, C and D. Luckily, as my car started shaking, I saw a sign to an area I knew, pulled off, and literally rolled into a station. Aquadonis (yes, I name my car) came through, and I laughed all the way home with a sense of accomplishment.

This same routine may be hindering what you can achieve. Looking to lose weight but still eating the same? Build muscle but doing the same program? Find adventure but always walking the same direction? Better sleep but the same evening routine? We can challenge our brains in gentle ways:

- Change a route on your walk, drive or commute.
- Change your morning beverage for one day.
- Move the positions of your phone apps one space to the left.
- Switch the order of two tasks in your morning routine.
- Put your shoes on the opposite foot first, or switch whether pants or shirt go on first.

Have a think about your own routine. What can you shake up to reset, rethink, or reframe a habit that's so ingrained? What is one thing you can switch to make your brain stop, think and recalibrate? I'd love to know what you switch up this week!

Until next week,
Nikki
