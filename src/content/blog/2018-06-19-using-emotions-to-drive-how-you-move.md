Hey,

Life isn't linear! It's not all puppies and rainbows. It can be great, but curveballs can be thrown left, right and centre, and we need to learn to adjust, deal with it, and keep on trucking.

Awareness is knowing what's happening around you. Self-awareness is the ability to know what we're doing as we do it, and to understand why. A great deal of your decisions are informed by your emotional responses, because that's what emotions are designed to do: appraise and summarise an experience, and inform your actions.

Think about it. How many times has an emotion been triggered, and your body just reacted in a certain way it has for many years? Have you just been through a breakup and reached for your comfort food? Something stressing you out and you just felt like running? Had a disagreement that left you wanting to smash a boxing or strength session? Emotions are powerful — they can enhance you or derail you, depending on the situation and your ability to see the outcome of your decision.

Everyone deals with emotions differently, and that's okay. Use the emotional fuel to interpret how your body may benefit from movement at that time. If you're angry, you may need to lash out in a controlled environment like a boxing or strength session — or a calming bodyweight restorative session may be what you need. Either is totally fine. Take a moment to realise the situation and adapt your session to how you feel you want to move.

Emotions can sneak up on us. There could be a rough time of year where memories automatically get conjured, or you hear a particular song or smell a childhood smell. For me, June is really hard. It's my birthday month, yet also when my big sister passed away suddenly — eight years ago today. This time of year can go either way depending on my hormones. I can feel strong and indestructible, knowing that with everything that's happened I'm still here, trying to live the best life I can — I can lift strong, punch hard, set new PBs. Alternatively, the energy can be zapped right out of me and all I can handle is bodyweight movement, if that. I crave hugs and comfort food (what we snacked on as kids) and just want to curl in a ball.

We all go through stages or events that help define us. Using the power of emotion, and really tapping into why we're reaching for something, can have a major impact on how we deal with the same situation again. We all have a history that can be re-written. Learning why you're reacting the way you do can be super powerful.

Pay attention to what your body needs and has the energy for. Not all effective workouts need to leave you a sweaty mess, unable to walk the next day. The takeaways: tune in and pay attention — you may uncover traits you weren't aware of. Move how your body can handle at the time. Use emotions to fuel how you want to move (although be realistic — if you're forever feeling angry and lashing out, maybe there's something else you need to change in daily life). And move to see what your body can do, not to punish it for what you may have done.

Cheers until next time,
Nikki
