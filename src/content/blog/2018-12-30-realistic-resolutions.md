Hey,

Are you in a progressive or a failure mindset?

So 2018 is coming to an end. For some it may have been an amazing year, others maybe not, others a mixture of both. No matter what type of year you think you've had, how about taking a moment to realise and appreciate all that you actually have done in a positive way.

For example, my movement goal this year was to do a full chin-up from a dead hang (my whole bodyweight hanging from the bar, rather than the jump-up I needed thus far). Have I achieved it yet? No. Now, I can look at this two ways. One: "OMG I'm such a failure, I suck at this, I'm never going to get to the end goal" — sound familiar? Or two: I can give myself an epic high five at how far I've come. I may not be there yet, but I've overcome shoulder pain and can attempt a chin-up without shooting pain down to my elbow. Am I feeling more in control of the movement? Yes. Have I progressed from where I started? Yes. All of these are mini achievements I'm damn proud of.

Take a moment to really think about what you've achieved this year. And I don't want to hear "nothing"! If you're struggling to remember, scroll through your camera roll for a refresh. You may not be at the end goal you envisioned yet, but well done for starting that thing you have.

So here we are, NYE 2018. It's this time of year we proclaim the "new and improved me": I'll lose weight, get fit, go to the gym four times a week, eat only healthy, spend more time with family, create work-life balance. Huge revelations that are motivating for, what, a week? Maybe a month, then old habits creep in — the original habits that got you wanting to change in the first place. Reality check: if you can't understand the detrimental habits that got you into this state of mind, it's never going to change.

Take "2019 is the year I'll lose weight". Before proclaiming what society tells you that you need, think: what habits added this in the first place? What are you doing, or not doing, on a daily or weekly basis that just isn't serving your body positively? This is the key to unlocking your change — understanding what got you to this stage. This awareness is vital if you want the change to stick long-term.

January in the fitness industry is like a pack of vultures ready to pounce. The feeds are about to be inundated with pills, shakes, meal replacements and skinny tea. C'mon — you're not that gullible, are you? Say you give in to a "wonder" product and lose the weight. What comes next? What did you learn about yourself so you won't return to that original state? Chances are you've simply masked over the original issues, and once you're off the product and back in your real-world routine, old habits return and the weight creeps back.

Realising your own triggers isn't easy, but it's vital for long-lasting change, no matter the goal. Make it realistic — try not to set yourself up for a feeling of failure from the beginning. Once you've figured out merely one detrimental habit, take a small, realistic step to change it. If you want to exercise four times a week but haven't been physical in a long time, NYE isn't a magic wand. Find what you can realistically start with, achieve that mini goal for two weeks, be proud of it, then look at how to increase it the following week.

Whatever goals you intend to kick in 2019, realise why you want them, and think about how achieving or progressing towards them will make you feel.

So off you toddle. Go on, get kicking!

Until next time,
