Hey,

Let's have a little Sunday chat about *balance* — not just the kind you need for single leg deadlifts or walking barefoot across pebbles like a ninja — I'm talking about the life kind. The *practise-what-you-preach* kind.

The past few weeks, I've thrown myself into the student seat — not just to stretch the ol' hamstrings or ego — but because yes, *even coaches need coaches*. I'm currently being coached by Coach Pauly and Coach Choc, and no, that's not a hip-hop duo from the 90s (although I'd 100% go to that concert).

Why? Because I know I'm human. And being human means I come with a cocktail of strengths, blind spots, and the occasional tendency to avoid the "needs work" section of my own training. Sure, I *can* write a program for myself. But am I going to fill it with the juicy stuff I *should* be doing… or the fun stuff I *like* to do? (Spoiler: I'd 100% Animal Flow and vibe-check my way through the week.)

Having someone else take a proper look at my movement — not just what I think I look like in the mirror — gives me perspective. The kind of honest insight that's hard to get from a self-assessment. (Especially when you're dancing between sets and calling it "active rest.")

And here's the thing: a great coach isn't just someone who yells "one more rep!" while scrolling Instagram. They're someone who:

- **Knows their stuff** — expertise matters. You want someone who knows the science *and* the art.
- **Can actually see you** — not just your reps, but your patterns, your pitfalls, and your potential.
- **Holds the vision** — when you can't see the forest for the foam rollers, your coach can.
- **Keeps you accountable** — because "I'll start Monday" is how we got here in the first place.
- **Creates safety and trust** — you can't push limits if you don't feel safe to stumble first.

Coach Pauly and Coach Choc tick all those boxes for me. They're skilled, trustworthy, and create a session that feeds my brain as much as it does my body — a place where I'm learning, growing, and swapping ideas with other curious minds.

And that idea of *accountability*? It's not just for the gym. Whether it's nutrition, business goals, mental health, or just remembering to close your damn laptop by 9pm — home accountability counts too. It's *always* okay to ask for help, no matter the topic.

So this week's little nudge is this: if you've been trying to balance it all on your own, maybe it's time to let someone else help you carry the load. Because whether you're a coach, a newbie, or somewhere in between — we all do better with the right support crew in our corner.

Here's to practising what we preach, and letting ourselves be students, too.

Stay Sassy,
Nikki
