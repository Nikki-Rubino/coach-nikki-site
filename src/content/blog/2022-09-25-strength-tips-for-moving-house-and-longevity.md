Hi Friend,

Anyone who's moved house without the luxury of removalists knows it's one of the toughest workouts out there. There's lifting, pushing, pulling, up and down stairs, reaching high, and getting heavy things down from overhead — all while navigating corners, losing your peripheral vision, and contorting yourself in ways you didn't think possible. Not to mention the mental load of organising, packing, and booking trucks.

> Train to control your body for real-world experiences and longevity.

Moving house is *not* an optimal training environment, so learn how to brace and control through all your angles ahead of time.

**Strengthen your trunk.** This is your main bracing force — and it doesn't mean endless sit-ups or crunches. Most of our lives are already spent in flexion. Incorporate all sides of your trunk with moves like the Underswitch and Superman.

**Strengthen quads and hamstrings.** There'll be a lot of loaded squat and hinge variations — you'll be picking things up and down off the floor and walking with them. Mix squats, split squats, lunge variations (including side variations), and step-ups, varying your grips and loaded positions (rack, farmer's carry, overhead). Hinge variations: deadlifts with split and sumo stance, Romanian deadlifts (double and single leg), and good mornings to bulletproof the posterior chain.

**Get your glutes ready.** They'll take a beating. Prime them with banded X-walks, frog pumps, and hip thrusts.

**Bulletproof your shoulders.** Overhead pressing in various grips is a must. Your shoulders have four ranges — horizontal push and pull, vertical push and pull. Cover them with overhead press, prone lat pulldown, chest press, and bent-over row.

**Arms.** You'll be carrying a truckload, mostly under load in a bent position. Bicep curls and tricep pulldowns help, and learn to use your back in all push and pull scenarios — ring rows, seated band rows, eccentric push-ups, scap push-ups.

**Wrists.** These take a beating too. Keep them strong and grip-ready — grab a stress ball and squeeze it throughout your day, and learn a few wrist-release positions.

One thing I'm grateful for is a body that handled the move from a second-floor apartment, to a second-floor storage unit, to a new house with stairs front and back. I'm chuffed that — with my dad as witness — I carried a small washing machine up five stairs using only his eyes to tell me how many steps I had left. Understanding how to control my body meant I could do it unassisted, without risking injury to Dad, who desperately wanted to help.

These tips aren't purely for moving house — they're a great foundation for moving with purpose for decades to come. If you'd like help training for longevity, reach out. I'd love to chat about how to start your journey, or bring you back to routine after time off.

Keep moving and grooving,
Nikki
