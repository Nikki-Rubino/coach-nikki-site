Hey,

Thank you for replying with all of the base positions you realised last week. I had many enquiries: I sleep on my stomach, I'm a desk sitter, my hobbies put me in the same positions as work. So first of all, awareness is key! Now that you're aware of these positions, we can start to combat them with the opposite.

**Belly sleepers**

The mattress is a soft surface. If you're sleeping on your belly, your lower back starts to bear the brunt as the weight of your body sinks into the mattress and your spine enters extension (think bending backwards).

- Tip one: try sleeping with a pillow under your belly and hips. This keeps the lower back in a neutral position. Will the pillow stay there the whole night? Probably not, but it will start to settle things into a neutral position for as long as it can.
- Tip two: reverse the position by going into spinal flexion (rounding forward) to help unlock the tailbone and lumbar spine. Two movements that help are Wave Unloads and Billy Elliots.

(Side note: it will be the opposite for back sleepers — you can start by sleeping with a pillow underneath the backs of your knees. The movements above will still help you out too.)

**Desk workers**

I feel everyone has a bout of this, even if not a desk worker — most people find themselves in a seated position more often than not. In the car, on the couch, or at a desk (I hear you, puzzle peeps, and even I'm a paint-by-numbers fan). The hip flexors can get jacked up in this seated position, spine flexed with a forward head posture.

- Tip one: get up! Stand up and go for a walk, even if it's just to the other side of the room. If you're driving, of course it's different — you can reverse your forward head posture at each set of traffic lights. Tuck your chin and push the back of your head into the headrest for five to 10 seconds. You should feel the muscles in the back of your neck and upper back start to fire up.
- Tip two: open up your hip flexors and rotate the spine. A foam roller hip extension and a hip 90/90 with rotational twist, done one after another, can help bring some love back into your whole hip capsule. A bonus move I give all my seated working clients is the Crab Reach, for all skill levels.

Let me know if any of these resonate, and share with your friends if they're in need of some movement inspo too.
